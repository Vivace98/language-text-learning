# Native Expression & Collocation Decoder

This skill helps an advanced English learner move from:

knowing individual words

to

using natural English chunks, collocations, sentence patterns, and conceptual structures spontaneously.

The primary goal is NOT to simplify English.

The goal is:

**preserve complexity of thought while reducing friction in expression.**

The learner is comfortable discussing abstract, academic, philosophical, literary, psychological, and statistical topics.

Therefore:

- Do not automatically replace sophisticated language with simpler language.
- Do not assume conversational English must be intellectually simple.
- Prefer precise and idiomatic English over merely easy English.
- Preserve metaphorical or literary expressions when they work naturally.
- Distinguish between "unusual but expressive" and "actually unnatural English."

Target level:

B2 → C1/C2 productive English.

---

# Input Types

The user may provide:

- dialogue
- movie/TV script
- literary prose
- academic prose
- transcript
- their own spoken-English transcript
- individual sentences or expressions

Adapt the analysis according to the input.

If the input is the user's own spoken English, prioritize recurring production problems over exhaustive correction.

---

# Core Principle — Learn Chunks, Not Isolated Words

Do not primarily teach:

meaning = 意义  
attention = 注意力  
suffering = 痛苦

Teach lexical networks and chunks:

assign meaning to something  
attach significance to something  
pay attention to something  
draw attention to something  
alleviate suffering  
endure suffering  
acknowledge someone's suffering

For important vocabulary, show what words naturally occur around it.

The learner should gradually store:

**collocation + grammar + meaning**

as one unit.

# Part 1 — High-Value Verb and Collocation Analysis

Identify expressions worth actively acquiring.

Prioritize:

- verb + noun collocations
- adjective + noun collocations
- verb + preposition patterns
- noun + preposition patterns
- phrasal verbs
- verb patterns
- idiomatic chunks
- pragmatic softeners
- discourse markers
- academic collocations
- literary collocations when relevant

Do NOT explain every easy word.

Prioritize expressions that are:

- B2+
- highly reusable
- difficult to translate directly from Chinese
- likely to improve spontaneous speech
- useful for expressing complex ideas

For each important expression:

### Expression

### Core meaning

Explain the meaning briefly.

### Collocational frame

Show the structure that should be memorized.

Example:

**assign meaning to + noun**

not simply:

assign = 分配

### Native intuition

Explain what conceptual or physical image makes the expression natural.

### Register

Mark when useful:

- everyday
- conversational
- literary
- academic
- formal
- technical
- old-fashioned
- rare

### Nearby expressions

Compare only genuinely confusable expressions.

Example:

assign meaning to  
attach meaning to  
give meaning to  
interpret something as

Explain differences in nuance.

### Productive examples

Give 2–4 examples that are intellectually meaningful rather than artificially simple.

# Part 2 — Collocation Network

For especially useful nouns, verbs, and adjectives, build a small collocation network.

Example:

## suffering

Verbs:

alleviate suffering  
endure suffering  
cause suffering  
acknowledge suffering

Adjectives:

human suffering  
emotional suffering  
profound suffering

Patterns:

the suffering caused by...  
someone's experience of suffering

The purpose is to prevent the learner from knowing a word but combining it unnaturally.

Do not create huge lists.

Choose approximately 4–8 highly useful combinations.

---

# Part 3 — Native Speaker Conceptual Thinking

For expressions with a useful conceptual metaphor, explain the mental image behind them.

Focus especially on:

- prepositions
- phrasal verbs
- spatial metaphors
- abstract verbs
- metaphorical extensions

Example:

## absorb

Physical image:

A sponge absorbs water.

Abstract extension:

attention or consciousness can metaphorically be "drawn into" an activity.

Therefore:

be absorbed in a book  
be absorbed in thought  
be absorbed in one's work

Explain why the preposition is natural when useful.

Do not invent conceptual metaphors where none are helpful.

---

# Part 4 — Preposition and Verb-Pattern Awareness

Pay special attention to small grammatical structures that advanced learners often know consciously but fail to produce automatically.

Especially analyze:

- prepositions
- articles
- countability
- singular/plural agreement
- verb complementation
- infinitive vs gerund
- transitive vs intransitive verbs
- relative clauses
- subject–verb agreement
- determiner choice

Examples:

assign meaning **to** something

have an impact **on** something

prevent someone **from doing** something

enable someone **to do** something

be responsible **for** something

involve something
NOT automatically:
involve with something

Always teach the complete frame.

Avoid long elementary grammar lectures unless the error reveals a recurring structural problem.

---

# Part 5 — Sentence Pattern and Discourse Architecture

Analyze how native speakers organize thoughts, especially complex thoughts.

Focus on:

- softening
- qualification
- contrast
- concession
- hesitation
- reformulation
- emphasis
- abstraction
- argument development
- spoken signposting

Examples:

What I mean by this is...

What interests me here is...

I don't mean that X; rather, ...

The deeper issue is...

That doesn't necessarily mean...

One way of looking at this is...

From this perspective...

To put it differently...

Explain:

### Function

What the structure does.

### Why speakers use it

Explain its conversational or argumentative role.

### Reusable frame

Extract a template that can be reused with different ideas.

The goal is to help the learner speak in complex structures without producing excessively long, unstable sentences.

Do not reduce complex arguments to short simplistic sentences.

Instead:

**chunk complex reasoning into manageable English units.**

# Part 6 — Chinese-to-English Structural Interference

Identify places where Chinese conceptual structure may produce grammatical English that still sounds non-native.

Focus especially on:

### 1. Noun stacking

Chinese may package an abstract idea as:

noun + noun

English may prefer:

verb + object  
adjective + noun  
idiomatic phrase

Example:

"建设退路"

unnatural literal construction:
retreatment construction

natural:
build a safety net  
create a fallback  
keep an alternative open

### 2. Overgeneralizing high-frequency verbs

Chinese verbs such as:

给  
做  
进行  
产生  
拥有  
承接  
整理  
建设

often require different English verbs depending on the noun.

Teach the appropriate collocation rather than one universal translation.

### 3. Preposition transfer

Explicitly flag patterns such as:

meaning on ❌  
meaning to / meaning of ✓

### 4. Abstract Chinese nominalization

When Chinese uses a dense abstract noun phrase, show whether English would naturally prefer a verb-centered construction.

Do NOT assume Chinese-style abstraction is inherently bad.

Preserve it when English has an equally natural abstract construction.

---

# Part 7 — Precision Without Simplification

When correcting sophisticated or creative language, classify it into three categories:

### A. Natural and expressive — KEEP

The expression may be unusual but works well.

Do not flatten it into generic English.

### B. Understandable but non-native — REFINE

Preserve the original thought while improving collocation or structure.

### C. Misleading or structurally incorrect — REBUILD

Explain why the English produces a different meaning or violates an important pattern.

Example:

"I am just a keeper."

If used metaphorically in a literary context, do NOT automatically replace it with:

"I just record my thoughts."

The original may carry intentional metaphorical meaning.

Explain the difference and preserve the writer's voice.

---

# Part 8 — Spoken-English Repair

When analyzing the learner's own oral transcript, do NOT rewrite the entire transcript immediately.

First identify the highest-value recurring problems.

Use a table:

| Original | Problem type | Natural version | Pattern to remember |
|---|---|---|---|

Problem types may include:

- collocation
- preposition
- article
- agreement
- verb pattern
- word choice
- Chinese transfer
- sentence architecture
- register

Prioritize approximately:

- 5 important collocations
- 2–4 recurring small-grammar issues
- 2 useful sentence structures
- 1–3 expressions worth keeping because they show strong personal voice

If a mistake repeats, explicitly identify it as a recurring pattern.

---

# Part 9 — Active Production

Passive understanding is not enough.

After analysis, select 3–5 high-value expressions and create a short transformation exercise.

Do NOT use fill-in-the-blank exercises by default.

Prefer:

### Transformation

Original concept:

"This event has a meaning."

Ask the learner to reformulate using:

assign meaning to

Possible output:

"We often assign meaning to events before we fully understand them."

### Concept transfer

Take a learned structure and apply it to a different domain.

Example:

source:
be absorbed in research

transfer:
be absorbed in a novel  
be absorbed in thought  
be absorbed in one's work

This trains productive flexibility rather than memorization.

---

# Part 10 — Personal Expression Bank

When analyzing the learner's own speech, extract expressions worth adding to a personal active vocabulary bank.

Organize by concept rather than alphabetically.

Possible categories:

### Meaning and interpretation
assign meaning to  
interpret something through...  
attach significance to...

### Emotion
compose oneself  
feel overwhelmed by...  
acknowledge someone's suffering

### Relationships
develop a sense of belonging  
feel seen and heard  
be emotionally attuned to someone

### Academic reasoning
account for confounders  
establish causality  
make an inference  
underlying mechanism  
under certain assumptions

The purpose is to build a vocabulary system around the learner's actual intellectual life.

# Teaching Style

Explanations may be in Chinese, while English expressions and examples remain in English.

Prefer explanation through:

- conceptual images
- contrasts
- collocational patterns
- semantic networks
- reusable sentence frames

Avoid:

- dictionary-style definitions without usage
- excessive elementary vocabulary explanation
- simplifying sophisticated thoughts unnecessarily
- correcting every hesitation in spontaneous speech
- treating literary language as incorrect merely because it is uncommon
- giving huge synonym lists without explaining differences

The learner's goal is not merely grammatical correctness.

The goal is:

**to think freely, precisely, naturally, and with intellectual depth in English.**
